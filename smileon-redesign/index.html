01 · Market snapshot

Dubai dentistry is crowded, review-driven and priced in wide ranges

Patients in Jumeirah, Satwa and Al Wasl compare clinics on three things: Google rating and review volume, whether a specialist will do the work, and whether a price is shown. Big groups win on volume of reviews and locations. Single clinics win when they look more specialised and more transparent.

03 · Patterns on competitors' service pages

What the best-converting implant and service pages have in common

Patterns worth adopting

  • Price near the top. Le Denté and the aggregators show a price table. Patients filter on price before they read further.
  • A guarantee or reassurance. Le Denté offers a 10-year implant warranty and Rex promotes 3D-guided surgery.
  • Named doctor on each service. Dr. Michael's links every service to a dentist's profile.
  • Area landing pages. Rex targets "Satwa" and "Jumeirah" directly.
  • Visible review volume next to every call to action.

Where Smile On can win

  • Eight specialists in one clinic. Big groups spread specialists across branches. Smile On has them in one building, so make that the headline.
  • A 5.0 rating. Competitors sit at 4.7–4.9. Smile On has fewer reviews, but a higher score.
  • Openness about same-day loading. The clinic's "we only load if stability passes" rule builds trust that competitors don't offer.
  • A published AED 150 cleaning price. It's an easy first booking that brings patients in.
  • Open until 9pm, which suits people who work in Jumeirah and Satwa.

05 · Redesign rationale

Why the three pages are built the way they are

Homepage

  • The hero's message, "Eight dental specialists. One clinic.", is the one claim no nearby competitor can match.
  • A trust strip (5.0 rating, 8 specialties, DHA, insurance) appears before any scrolling.
  • Implants get a large card because they're the highest-value treatment. The other 13 services sit in a scannable grid.
  • A "Know the cost" price board shows AED 150, 499 and 4,500+.
  • A sticky Call and Book bar on mobile, where most Dubai dental searches happen.

Services hub and implant page

  • The hub opens with a symptom chooser ("My tooth hurts", "It's for my child") so patients don't need to know department names.
  • Each department shows its lead doctor, which answers "who will treat me?"
  • The implant page has a day-one timeline, six real steps, a safety note on loading, a price table and FAQs written for search snippets.
  • Each design uses one accent colour, saved for the booking button so it's always the clearest thing to click.
06 · Before launch

Checklist for the developer and the clinic

  • Confirm one set of hours, years of experience, the doctor roster and the prices. These are flagged in yellow in each design.
  • Replace the review placeholders with real Google reviews, quoted exactly and with permission.
  • Shoot real photos: the treatment room, reception, doctor portraits and a CBCT scan in use. Don't use stock images of other clinics.
  • Add LocalBusiness/Dentist, FAQPage, MedicalProcedure and Physician schema that matches the confirmed facts.
  • Consolidate the duplicate service URLs with 301 redirects, then point internal links at the new hub.
  • Check claims and before and after imagery against DHA healthcare advertising rules.
